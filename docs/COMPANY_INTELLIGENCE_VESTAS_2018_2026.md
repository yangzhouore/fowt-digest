## Coverage limits

- The denser 2018–2020 years reflect dedicated offshore archives. The lower 2021, 2025 and partial-2026 counts were rechecked against the official publication index, order table and developer/contractor reports. Coverage of routine installation and service milestones, particularly local-language announcements in Japan, South Korea and Taiwan, remains uneven. Public announcements, anonymous contracts and unpublished orders have different visibility. Counts describe reviewed public records in this audit.
- Only the two documented MHI Vestas digital developments cleared the scope screen. Later project monitoring, autonomous inspection and broad turbine-service material did not independently establish a distinct Vestas offshore digital deployment. The existing Digital & AI Signals were checked for explicit Vestas participation and were not used as proxies for an OEM’s work.
- Corporate results were useful as pointers to projects and dates. Generic earnings, share transactions, buybacks, staffing notices, routine ceremonies, onshore agreements, generic marketing and unattributable or undated claims were excluded. A known Vestas supplier relationship alone was insufficient to include each developer announcement. Primary sources sometimes block automated clients or change region/locale URLs.

## Event quality review

- **One consolidation.** Vestas’s 17 June 2024 Nordlicht conditional agreement already specified low-emission steel for sections of 56 of 112 towers. Vattenfall’s 24 June release restated that commitment in the same turbine package. The Vestas announcement leads the shared event, and the developer release provides supporting evidence. Nordlicht I’s later firm order is a distinct event.
- **Orders and supply chains.** The two 295 MW Taiwan orders on 31 March 2022 had different customers and projects. Vanguard East and West are separate orders for separate phases. The 2019 Swancor and switchgear announcements covered conditional agreements. Their 2020 releases recorded purchase agreements with the same counterparties. The event summaries now identify the earlier stage explicitly. Szczecin’s blade-plant proposal and pause concern a different facility from its nacelle project.
- **Installation and operation.** For EFGL, assembly in port, offshore installation, first grid power and full power are separate milestones. At Kincardine, the first V80 installation predates the order and harbour assembly of the V164 units. Boston’s January site selection and June office opening are also separate steps. The Neretek trade report supports the same memorandum covered by the lead primary release.
- **Historical attribution.** The archive body governs the Boston record despite the mismatched headline. Former joint-venture attribution stays tied to the canonical Vestas ID. The distinct MHI Vestas Japan venture, the acquisition and post-acquisition integration keep their documented identities.

## Static Company Intelligence event contract

`web/data/context/company-events.json` is the shared `company-events.v1` catalogue. It replaces the Vestas-only history and the per-company source link list. Each event has a global stable `id`, the lead citation’s `publishedDate`, a title, a factual summary, one controlled category, a review date, distinct `companyIds`, zero or more distinct `projectIds`, and at least one source. The backfill window applies to this review. The catalogue accepts later publications.

- **Company pages.** Add a reviewed canonical Industry ID to an event’s `companyIds` to make that event discoverable under another company. The adapter indexes the same resolved event under each linked ID and sends only the timeline fields to each page. A fixture verifies the same Vestas–Equinor event is shared by both indexes. Published links are scoped to the Vestas review.
- **Citations.** The first citation determines the timeline date and original-source link. Put a primary citation first when available. External sources carry the original URL, publisher and tier. The lead external source uses the reviewed summary as evidence unless a separate note is supplied. Supporting external sources carry their own evidence and can specify a publication date. Engineering citations reference an edition slug and source record ID. Digital & AI citations reference a Signal and its source ID. These references reuse retained metadata and require an exact stored excerpt. A publication can support several distinct events. Validation rejects repeated event IDs and duplicate citations within an event. Detecting duplicate developments across different publications remains an editorial check.
- **Projects and historical names.** Event project links use canonical IDs backed by identifying evidence. Verified company–project roles continue to drive the separate Related Projects section. An optional attribution records the historical name against a linked company ID and preserves the original publisher.
- **Future search.** Index by event ID, join to Industry and Projects by ID, and follow exact Engineering and Digital & AI references. Research keeps its canonical paper IDs. Future direct event-to-paper links would use explicit reviewed paper references.
- **Future subscriptions.** Given selected company IDs and categories, match any selected ID and any selected category, apply a date window, and deduplicate by event ID. A development shared by two selected companies then appears once.
